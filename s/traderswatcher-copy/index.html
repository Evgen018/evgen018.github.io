Sample, written before the brief

TradersWatcher landing copy

Six sections of a full landing page, written from your live site and the product itself, so you can judge the writing before you hire anybody.

The angle: your reader does not need to be told that journaling matters. He already knows, and his journal is still empty. That gap is where the page starts.

01   Hero

Your journal is empty. Your broker statement is not.

TradersWatcher records every fill, every chart at entry and every word you say about the trade, then puts what you meant to do next to what you actually did. The review you keep promising yourself happens on its own, on Friday, whether you had eight seconds between setups or eight hours.

Start watching yourself trade. 7 days free, no card.

Read-only connection. It can never place an order. Works with MT4, MT5, crypto exchanges, prop firms and any CSV.

Designer: one screen, one promise. The product shot belongs next to the headline, not below the fold, and it should show a real session with a real loss in it. A green-only screenshot reads as a demo and traders spot it in half a second.

02   The problem

The trades you never wrote down are the ones costing you.

You know the rule. Log every trade, review at the weekend, find the leak.

You have also had eight seconds between setups since the London open, and the only thing you logged today is a screenshot you will not recognise on Sunday.

So the review never happens. And the leak stays exactly where it is: not in your strategy, but in the four minutes after a loss, when you size up to win it back.

Nobody sees that trade. Not your broker, who sees a fill and a timestamp. Not the spreadsheet, which knows the number and nothing around it. Not the journal app you paid for in January and opened twice.

You see it. Once a month, on the statement. You just cannot prove where it came from, so you change the strategy instead of the habit, and start again.

Designer: this block earns its keep as text. Give it room, one column, generous line height, no icons. The only visual that helps here is an empty journal table with the cursor blinking in row one.

03   How it actually works

It watches the trade you meant to take, and the trade you took.

Before the open you answer five questions. Mood, sleep, the headlines you have already read, your goal for the day, your max risk per trade. Sixty seconds, with your coffee.

Then you trade the way you always trade. TradersWatcher takes the fills straight from your terminal, captures the chart at entry, records the news and the session around it, and keeps your voice note attached to the trade it belongs to.

At the end of the day those two things get read together: what you said you would do, and what the tape says you did.

That gap is the whole product. A manual journal can never show it to you, because you write a manual journal from memory, and memory edits.

By the end of the first week it can tell you:

  • which three of your setups actually pay you, ranked by edge and recalculated every night
  • the hour of the day where your win rate quietly falls apart
  • whether your most confident entries are really your best ones, measured instead of felt
  • what your month looks like if you stop after three losses, recomputed on your own trades and not on a demo account
  • how much of your last drawdown was your decision, and how much was slippage, spread and fill latency

Designer: three numbered steps across, then the list as a single column underneath. The list is the part people screenshot and send to a trading group, so it needs to survive being cropped.

04   For prop traders

Day 8 of 30. Up 3.8 percent. One trade from zero.

A challenge is rarely lost on a bad read. It is lost on a rule nobody had time to count. Daily loss limit, total drawdown, both trailing types, news windows, position size: five numbers to hold in your head while you are reading price.

TradersWatcher holds them instead, recalculated from your terminal with floating positions included. One trade from a breach, it says so early and in plain words.

It warns you. It never freezes your platform and it never touches your orders. The decision stays yours, with the numbers in front of you.

At the end of the period there is one document for your firm: trades, rule adherence, timestamps.

Designer: the live tracker widget is the hero image of this block. Show it at 68 percent of the daily limit, in the warning state. Nobody is moved by a tracker that is comfortably in the green.

05   What you are probably thinking

I already have a journal. So does everyone who is still guessing. The question is not whether you have one, it is whether it was filled in on your worst day. That is the day worth reading.
I am not talking to my computer. Then do not. Voice is optional and it happens after the session, never during. Fills, charts and market context still feed the AI. You lose the emotion mapping and keep everything else.
Can it trade my account? No. The connection is read-only, so placing an order is not something it is able to do, with your permission or without it. Your data is encrypted, isolated to your account, and you can delete all of it whenever you want.
How is this different from TraderSync or Edgewonk? They record what you did. This records how you did it, then compares it with what you said you would do. For a scalper at 47 trades a day, that difference is the entire point.
What if I do not like it? Seven days of full access, no card. After that the journal itself stays free. You pay 29 dollars a month for the AI depth, 49 for the simulation layer, and there is a 14-day refund if you change your mind. Cancel in two clicks.

Designer: five questions, five short answers, closed by default on mobile. Objection blocks convert when they look honest and cheap, not when they look designed.
